To the Editor-in-Chief, *Artificial Intelligence Review*

**Re: Submission of a review article — "When Models Know They Are Being Tested: A Structured Review of Evaluation Awareness, Sandbagging and Related Model-Originated Threats to the Validity of Large Language Model Evaluation"**

Dear Editor,

We submit the above manuscript for consideration as a review article in *Artificial Intelligence Review*.

Decisions about deploying and governing large language models increasingly rest on evaluation results. A rapidly growing literature, most of it from 2025–2026, reports that models can recognise when they are being evaluated and may behave differently as a result. They may underperform on capability tests, appear safer on alignment tests, or mislead automated graders. These are threats that originate in the evaluated model itself rather than in benchmark design. To our knowledge they have not been synthesised as a single validity problem: existing reviews address AI deception broadly, benchmark construct validity, or evaluation–safety failures in general.

The manuscript contributes:

1. A documented evidence map of 225 sources, selected from 418 screened records, with all queries, screening decisions and counts released as reproducible scripts and data.
2. A four-layer framework (context signal, awareness, behavioural divergence, validity consequence) that separates constructs the literature frequently conflates.
3. A comparison of measurement methods with their validity evidence and documented failure modes, and a typed analysis of five apparent contradictions.
4. An evidence-graded research agenda and a twelve-item reporting checklist for evaluation papers and system cards.

We believe the work suits the journal's readership, which spans AI methods and their critical evaluation, because it addresses how the community knows what its models can do and how they behave.

The manuscript is transparent about its limitations. Because licensed bibliographic databases and full texts were not accessible in the environment used, records were identified through logged web searches and charted at abstract level. We therefore describe the work as a structured critical review with a scoping-style evidence map, not as a PRISMA-ScR-compliant scoping review. As set out in the AI-use statement, an AI system was used extensively to run and log searches, apply scripted screening and charting rules, generate figures, and draft text. The authors directed the work and take full responsibility for it.

This manuscript is original, has not been published previously, and is not under consideration for publication elsewhere. Both authors have approved the manuscript and agree with its submission. The authors declare no competing interests.
